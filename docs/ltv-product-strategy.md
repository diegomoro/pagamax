# Pagamax LTV product strategy

Date: 2026-05-24

Ultimate objective: maximize time-discounted net profit. LTV work should increase the present value of future revenue after acquisition, operations, support, fraud, legal/compliance, data, and trust-damage costs. The shopper experience matters because it creates the repeated trusted checkout intent that drives monetizable LTV.

Launch sequence: Cordoba first, then Rosario, then Argentina.

## Highest-LTV personas

Optimize for personas that increase discounted net profit, not only gross usage. A persona is attractive when purchase frequency, promo complexity, willingness to share, merchant/issuer value, low support burden, and retention outweigh acquisition and servicing costs.

### Primary persona: promo-aware household optimizer

Likely profile: 28-48, comfortable with QR payments, manages recurring household purchases, uses several wallets/cards, buys groceries/fuel/pharmacy often, and already suspects there is a better payment route but does not want to remember every promo.

Why this user has high LTV:

- High purchase frequency in categories where payment promos matter.
- Enough payment complexity to make recommendations repeatedly useful.
- Strong habit potential: scan before supermarket, pharmacy, fuel, and online checkout.
- Emotional reward is practical competence, not entertainment.
- Low churn if the app reliably saves time, money, and embarrassment at checkout.
- Social influence inside family and close friend groups: this person often tells others how to pay.

### Secondary personas

- Promo-savvy professional: 24-40, many wallets/cards, wants fast decisions and status from being financially sharp.
- Family helper: 35-60, helps parents/partner choose how to pay, values clarity and low-risk manual fallback.
- Social organizer: 25-45, buys for groups or advises friends, likely to share if the product feels useful and not spammy.
- Small merchant/customer hybrid: understands QR payments and can become an early validator, but should not dominate consumer UX.

### Do not optimize for first

- Users with one payment method and low shopping frequency.
- Extreme deal hunters who want every condition exposed immediately.
- Users uncomfortable with QR payments or banking apps.
- Status-seeking users who want loud flexing, points, streaks, or fake exclusivity.
- People expecting Pagamax to automate or approve payments.

## UX principles used

- Make scanning the reflex: home now says "Antes de pagar, escanea" and keeps QR as the dominant action.
- Show one confident answer first: recommendation card labels the best route and confidence before alternatives.
- Preserve trust: manual fallback stays explicit when wallet handoff is not verified.
- Reduce mental load: onboarding delays optional settings until the last step and explains the core behavior in one sentence.
- Reinforce identity quietly: success and home frame the user as someone who pays intelligently, without gamification.
- Make sharing socially safe: share copy is about helping someone choose better, not flexing savings.
- Support category expansion: language stays broad enough for groceries, fuel, pharmacy, online checkout, and later travel.

## Friction removed

- Home no longer starts cold with only a button; it now gives a clear pre-payment habit cue.
- Results fallback no longer leads with a negative "Sin promo confirmada" headline; it says "Ruta por defecto" and explains why.
- Confidence labels are in Spanish in the UI.
- Scan screen tells the user what to do while the camera is open.
- Onboarding has less up-front configuration and a clearer final action.

## Habit loops improved

- Home shows monthly net savings and useful checks, reinforcing the routine without streaks.
- Frequent merchants are framed as atajos, encouraging repeat use.
- Success offers saving the merchant for reuse and a calm next-payment CTA.
- Returning from a wallet now has a clear "Ya volvi a Pagamax" path.

## Virality and status

Added:

- Subtle home share action: "Ayudar a alguien a pagar mejor."
- Success share action: "Compartir con alguien."
- Copy frames the user as financially competent and calm.

Avoided:

- Loud referral prompts.
- Fake exclusivity.
- Streaks, points, rankings, gambling-like rewards, or exaggerated savings flexes.

## Self-critique

- Busy parent: the main action is obvious, but real-world checkout speed still depends on camera scan reliability and wallet login friction.
- Older user: copy is clearer, but some QR/payment-app terms may still need validation in live interviews.
- Promo-savvy heavy saver: alternatives and details are present, but advanced filtering by bank/card/promo cap remains limited.
- Socially influential user: sharing is tasteful, but the app still needs a real referral destination before launch.

Most important next retention improvements:

1. Real merchant QR validation in Cordoba supermarkets, fuel stations, and pharmacies.
2. A launch-ready referral/link target instead of generic share text.
3. Better data freshness and visible "last checked" trust for local Cordoba promos.
4. Wallet-specific instructions learned from real confirmation-screen tests.
